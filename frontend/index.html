Frequently Asked Questions

Aircraft Maintenance Engineering (AME) is a specialised aviation program focused on the inspection, maintenance, troubleshooting and certification of aircraft and their systems. AMEs play a vital role in ensuring aircraft remain safe and airworthy.
AME offers career opportunities across airlines, aircraft MRO, component MRO, Non-Schedule operators, aircraft manufacturers, aircraft component manufactures, Flying Training Organization, CAR 147 (Basic) AME training institutes, Technical Writers companies, aircraft leasing organizations. Career progression depends on a candidate's training, experience, various aviation certification, aircraft type qualifications etc.
Candidates should have passed 10+2 with Physics, Chemistry and Mathematics (PCM) from a recognised board or an equivalent qualification (Diploma in Aeronautics /Mechanical/ Electricals/Electronics stream approved by state Technical Board).
B1.1 covers turbine-powered aeroplanes. Professionals in this category can pursue opportunities in airlines, MRO organisations, aircraft operators and other approved aviation maintenance organisations working with applicable turbine aeroplanes.
B1.2 covers piston-engine aeroplane. Career opportunities may include general aviation operators, aircraft maintenance organisations, flying training organisations and other organisations operating applicable piston-engine aeroplanes, subject to the required approvals and qualifications.
B1.3 covers turbine-powered helicopters. Professionals can pursue opportunities with helicopter operators, MRO organisations, aviation companies and other approved maintenance organisations operating applicable turbine helicopters.
B2 is the Avionics category, covering aircraft electrical, electronic, communication, navigation and instrument systems. B2 professionals can pursue opportunities with airlines, MROs, aircraft operators and other approved aviation maintenance organizations.
The duration depends on the approved training program and its structure. DGCA-approved basic training programs are structured according to the applicable CAR-147 (Basic) requirements. Generally, is of 24 months.
DGCA stands for Directorate General of Civil Aviation. It is the primary regulatory authority for civil aviation in India, functioning under the Ministry of Civil Aviation, Government of India.

The DGCA is headquartered in New Delhi, near Safdarjung Airport.
ICAO stands for the International Civil Aviation Organization. It is a specialized agency of the United Nations (UN) that develops and coordinates global standards for international civil aviation. It was established following the Chicago Convention and is headquartered in Montreal, Canada.

Simple Analogy: ICAO is a Global Rule-Maker for civil aviation.
IATA stands for the International Air Transport Association. It is the global trade association of airlines, representing more than 370 airlines and around 85% of worldwide air traffic.

IATA helps airlines operate smoothly using common industry standards.
FAA stands for the Federal Aviation Administration. It is the civil aviation regulator of the United States, operating under the U.S. Department of Transportation. Its mission is to provide the safest and most efficient aerospace ecosystem of the world.
